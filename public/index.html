Walls

How often the bot asks, and what it says.

How this works

Messages — each line of a message is sent as its own chat message, 1.5s apart, so you can pair a public call-out with a private status line. Placeholders: {minutes} unchecked, {player} who checked last, {checks} that player's count, {total} checks all-time. Outpost messages add {raidMinutes}, {raidPlayer}, {raidChecks} and {bot} (the wall account's name).

Listen on — dm (default) counts a trigger only when a player messages the bot privately, so a stray word in public chat can't fire it. chat reads public and faction chat instead. Anything else is treated as a custom /regex/ capturing the player name then the message.

Quiet hours — reminders stop, and in-game checks are refused with a note rather than counted. When the window lifts, the unchecked count restarts from zero, so the first message of the day isn't a number covering the whole night. The panel's Mark checked button still works as an operator override, and weewoo alerts are never gated — a raid is an emergency whenever it lands.

Quiet hours use the panel server's clock, not yours. A VPS usually runs on UTC — set TZ=America/New_York (or your zone) in .env and restart to make these times local.

How this works

Raid Outpost — set your faction and the bot follows the server's outpost broadcasts: claimed, 1-hour cooldown, then live. While it is live, players message the bot raid (or walls raid) and the reminder covers both. Grace period turns wall checks off entirely, so only the outpost is reminded; quiet hours mute walls but the outpost prompt still goes out.

How this works

Triggers accept a comma-separated word list, matched on whole words, or a /regex/ literal. Messages are sent exactly as written — start them with /f c to scope them to faction chat.

While a weewoo alert is running, wall reminders stop and resume when it's turned off. The Discord alert repeats on the interval below with a TNT icon, the raid headline, and a running time since alert; clearing it posts how long the alert lasted.

Discord

Webhooks for reminders, check logs and alarms.

How this works

Reminder embed body — Discord posts arrive as an embed with a coloured bar, a title and a timestamp. This field is the body of the reminder embed and supports markdown (**bold**), so it can be richer than the in-game line. Weewoo alerts use their own Weewoo Discord body.

Checks to a logs channel — every recorded check posts a log embed: the player's head and name, when the walls were confirmed clear, how long since the previous check, and their Raid Outpost/wall check counts. Left off, these follow the wall webhook; a blank URL falls back there too. The player head is fetched by Discord from mc-heads.net.

Weewoo webhook — off, weewoo alerts follow the wall webhook. On with a URL set, they go to that channel instead, so alarms can live somewhere noisier than routine wall checks. A blank URL falls back to the wall webhook rather than going nowhere.

Raid Outpost claimed/lost webhook — only the ownership news goes here: we claimed it, someone took it from us, our 12-hour hold ended. Leave it blank and that news falls back to the wall webhook. Raid Outpost reminders always go with the wall reminders.

Role ping — embeds can't ping, so the mention is sent as a line above the embed, limited to that one role. A weewoo alert pings on its first post only, not on every repeat. For the ping to land, the role must be mentionable (or the webhook allowed to mention it) in Discord.
